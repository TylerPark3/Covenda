    See how Covenda reduces oversight

    This already works.
    It just doesn’t scale.

    The best startups already hire undergraduates by asking someone who knows them. Covenda makes that repeatable — and gives the club or lab doing the vouching a record it owns.

    How it works today

    1. One founder emails two professors he happens to know.
    2. One professor names two students he happens to remember.
    3. One hire happens. Everyone else never hears about it.

    It works because someone credible vouched. It doesn’t scale because it runs on one person’s address book, and the club that produced the student gets nothing lasting from it.

    The same thing, systematised

    1. Your club vouches once. Members carry it into every application in your field.
    2. Covenda vets again — technically, against a published bar — so a company trusts it without knowing you.
    3. Every startup in that vertical can reach your students, not just the one founder with your email.
    4. Your club earns a record — admitted, verified, hired — that belongs to the club and outlives this year’s officers.

    The goal is not a bigger address book. It is letting a selective club anywhere build credibility that a founder who has never heard of it will still trust.

      Why now

      The talent is available.
      Nothing pairs it to the work.

      Startups can reach students big employers stopped hiring. The gap is not supply — it is knowing who will deliver, and on what.

      • 7%New grads are now just 7% of Big Tech hires, down by half since 2019. That supply is unclaimed.SignalFire State of Talent, 2025
      • 52%of new grads are underemployed — capable people paired to work that never tested them.Burning Glass Institute & Strada, 2024
      • 45%are still underemployed a decade on. A bad first pairing compounds; it does not self-correct.Burning Glass Institute & Strada, 2024
      • 50%of students would rather build something than climb a ladder. Startup work fits them — if it reaches them.Morning Consult for Samsung, 2023

      These figures cite published research, not Covenda results. Covenda has no outcome data yet — the pilot is how we earn it.

      Every ambitious student hits the same wall: “I could contribute here — I just can’t get in front of the founder.” What still works is what always worked: someone credible stakes their name on you, you get one shot, and the work proves the rest.

      Why Covenda

      The problem isn’t talent.
      It’s noise.

      AI made mass applications free, so whoever can actually deliver disappears into the pile. Covenda is the opposite bet: a short, vouched, reviewed list.

      The open pile

      • Thousands of look-alike applications, most of them AI-written
      • Founders and HR sift résumés no one wrote by hand
      • The students who’d actually deliver are invisible in the volume
      • Everyone competes on quantity — a race no one wins

      Covenda

      • A short list, vouched by a professor, club, or industry pro
      • Proven on a reviewed work-trial before it reaches you
      • Curated and selective on purpose; the vetting is the value
      • Judged on what they can do, not just where they studied

      How proof works

      Machines check facts.
      Experts judge work.

      Not a trade-off. A machine can prove an account is yours and that the history is real; it cannot tell you whether the thinking was any good. A person can judge the work but can't audit a commit log. Covenda runs both, and the strongest evidence is where they overlap.

      Machine-checked · objective

      Facts that hold up without anyone's opinion: who owns the account, when the work accumulated, whether the formulas compute.

        Expert-vetted · judgement

        What no API can answer: can they defend the decisions they made, and will someone who supervised them stake their name on it.

          Two raters score a walkthrough against published anchors and adjudicate where they disagree — judgement, measured. What never counts as proof is a simulation or a self-reported number.
