# LinkedIn outreach for Covenda

Written for the founder to send by hand. Nothing here is automated, and nothing here should be
sent in bulk — the whole pitch is that Covenda is not spray-and-pray, and outreach that
contradicts the pitch is worse than no outreach.

---

## The constraint that shapes everything

**There is no outcome data.** No student has completed a trial, no company has accepted a
deliverable. So every message below sells the *process* and asks for a *conversation*, never
results. The moment you write "we've placed X students" you have made the one claim a founder
will check, and you will lose the account you were trying to win.

What you can say truthfully today:
- How the vetting works, and that the bar is published
- That applications carry recorded work, not a résumé
- That the score is explainable and shows what it was compared on
- That you are early and looking for the first few companies

"We are early" is not a weakness in this pitch. Founders who hire interns off-cycle are used to
being someone's first customer, and being straight about it buys more trust than polish does.

---

## Who to target

LinkedIn Premium gives you search filters and InMail. Use the filters to make the list small,
not large.

### The company profile that actually converts

Not "startups". Specifically:

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **10–60 employees** | Big enough to have work piling up, small enough that the founder still owns hiring |
| **Seed to Series A** | Has money, has no recruiting function |
| **Posted an internship in the last 90 days** | Demonstrated intent. They already decided they want a student |
| **Job post still open after 30 days** | The strongest signal on this list. They tried and it did not work |
| **No in-house recruiter** | If they have one, you are a threat rather than a relief |

The fourth row is the one to build the list around. A founder with a stale internship post has
an active, admitted problem, and you are not interrupting them to create demand.

### Who to message inside it

**Message the founder or the hiring manager. Never HR.** At this size HR does not exist, and if
it does, it is a coordinator without budget. Titles that work: Founder, Co-founder, CEO, CTO,
Head of Engineering, Head of Ops, Chief of Staff.

Chief of Staff is underrated — they are usually the person who inherited "we need to hire
interns" and has no idea how.

## The message

### Rules

1. **Under 120 words.** InMail is read on a phone between meetings.
2. **One specific, verifiable detail** about them in the first sentence. Not "I love what you're
   building" — the exact role they posted, or a thing their product does.
3. **No link in the first message.** A link makes it an ad. Offer it in the reply.
4. **Ask for a reply, not a call.** "Worth a look?" converts better than "15 minutes Thursday?"
5. **Never send the same message twice.** If you cannot personalise it, do not send it.

### Template A — the stale job post

> Hi {First} — you posted for a {role} intern about {N} weeks ago and it looks like it is still
> open. That is usually not a sourcing problem, it is that a hundred résumés all read the same
> now.
>
> I run Covenda. Students apply with a recorded walkthrough and a screen recording of them
> working through an exercise we set, so you watch someone work before you meet them. We are
> early and picking the first few companies deliberately.
>
> Worth a look?

### Template B — no post, but obvious need

> Hi {First} — {specific thing about their product}. Curious whether you have tried bringing in
> students for {specific unglamorous task that is clearly not getting done}.
>
> I run Covenda. We vet undergraduates per discipline against a published bar and send you
> recorded work rather than a résumé. We have not placed anyone yet, so I am looking for a
> couple of companies willing to run the first one with me.
>
> Open to a look at how it works?

### Template C — warm, through a shared connection

> Hi {First} — {Mutual} mentioned you were hiring {role}. {One sentence on what they said}.
>
> I run Covenda, a vetting layer for undergraduate hiring. The short version: you describe the
> person you need, students apply with recorded work rather than a résumé, and you see why each
> one scored what they did.
>
> Happy to send the one-pager if useful.

### The follow-up

**One follow-up. Seven days. Then stop.**

> Hi {First} — following up once and then I will leave you alone. If the {role} search is
> already sorted, say the word and I will stop. If not, I would still value ten minutes of your
> read on whether this is useful.

That is the whole sequence. Two messages. A third makes you the thing the product is against.

## What to do when they reply

**"Interested, tell me more."** Send the one-pager and *one* concrete next step. Do not send a
deck.

**"What does it cost?"** Answer immediately and plainly. Evasion here kills more deals than any
number does.

**"How many students do you have?"** Say the real number. Then redirect to the thing that is
actually differentiated: "Fewer than you'd want. What I can show you is the vetting process and
exactly what an application looks like — that's the part that's different, and you can judge it
in five minutes."

**"Come back when you have traction."** Ask one question before you leave: *"Understood. What
would you need to see to try it?"* Their answer is worth more than the meeting was, and it is
the same question your product roadmap needs answered.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not use LinkedIn automation tools.** Beyond the account risk, you would be building the
  exact behaviour Covenda exists to fix. If it comes out later, it is the story about you.
- **Do not connect-then-pitch.** Send the InMail. The bait-and-switch connection request is the
  most disliked pattern on the platform.
- **Do not name-drop schools.** "Students from {university}" is the prestige proxy the product
  refuses everywhere else. Lead with the process.
- **Do not claim a partnership you do not have.** Not with clubs, not with universities, not
  with assessment platforms.
- **Do not send on Monday morning or Friday afternoon.** Tuesday to Thursday, 8–10am their time.

---

## Tracking

A spreadsheet is enough. Six columns: company, person, date sent, template, replied, outcome.

The number that matters early is **reply rate, not meeting rate**. A 15% reply rate on 40
carefully chosen people tells you the message works. A 2% reply rate on 400 tells you nothing
except that you sent 400 messages.

If reply rate is under 10% after 40 sends, the problem is the targeting, not the copy. Go back
to the stale-job-post filter.
