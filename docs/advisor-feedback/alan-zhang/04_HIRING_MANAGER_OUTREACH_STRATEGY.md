# Hiring-Manager Outreach Strategy

## Objective

Determine whether hiring managers experience a meaningful gap between the candidates their current process identifies and the candidates who could actually succeed on their teams.

## Working hypothesis

Quant software-engineering teams may value a system that identifies overlooked early-career candidates through difficult, role-relevant evidence. This is a hypothesis, not a final decision.

## Target groups

### Bar-setting interviews

Use highly selective quantitative trading firms, hedge funds, market makers, and systematic investment firms to understand the performance bar.

### Pilot-accessible organizations

Prioritize smaller funds, newer proprietary-trading firms, crypto market makers, financial-infrastructure startups, execution and trading-tool companies, alumni-led firms, and technical teams without massive campus programs.

## Target titles

- Engineering Manager
- Software Engineering Manager
- Head of Engineering
- CTO
- Quantitative Development Manager
- Research Engineering Manager
- Trading Systems Lead
- Infrastructure Engineering Lead
- Developer Productivity Lead
- Head of Quantitative Engineering
- Senior Engineer who mentors interns
- Columbia alumnus managing a technical team

## Warm-introduction hierarchy

1. Columbia alumni you know personally
2. Alumni one introduction away
3. Former interns at target firms
4. Professors and club leaders with industry ties
5. Founders and investors connected to target firms
6. Cold LinkedIn messages
7. Cold email

## Four-week target

- 25 personalized messages per week
- 10 warm-introduction requests per week
- 5 completed interviews per week
- 1 written synthesis every Friday

## Interview progression

- Interviews 1–5: learn vocabulary and patterns.
- Interviews 6–15: test whether the same capabilities and failures recur.
- Interviews 16–25: test a concrete simulation or work-product concept.
- Interviews 26–30: ask for pilot commitments from the strongest prospects.

## Validation threshold

Do not build a vertical-specific system until at least five managers independently confirm that existing screening misses meaningful candidates, resumes fail to show important capabilities, managers can define a nontrivial work product, they would review results, and the role recurs often enough to justify a reusable system.

## Pilot ask

> Help us define one difficult, safe, role-relevant work product. We will recruit a small group of strong students, administer it, verify the work, and return a structured set of results. You agree only to review the strongest submissions and provide feedback. No hiring commitment is required.
