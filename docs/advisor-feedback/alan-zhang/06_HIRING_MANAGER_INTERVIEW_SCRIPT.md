# Hiring-Manager Discovery Interview

## Opening

Thank you for taking the time. I am researching how teams evaluate undergraduate and early-career technical talent. I am not trying to sell a product during this call. I want to understand what predicts success, what current processes miss, and whether meaningful work can be represented safely before hiring.

## Questions

1. Tell me about the strongest intern or new graduate you have managed.
2. What did that person do differently in the first month?
3. What evidence of that ability was visible before hiring?
4. What became apparent only after the person joined?
5. Tell me about someone who looked strong during recruiting but struggled.
6. Which pre-hire signals were misleading?
7. Have you seen someone who looked ordinary on paper but became excellent?
8. Why did the normal process fail to recognize them?
9. What evidence could have revealed their ability earlier?
10. Are there candidates your firm systematically misses?
11. What tasks or decisions distinguish strong interns from average ones?
12. Which parts of the role could be simulated safely outside the firm?
13. What would make a student project credible rather than performative?
14. How long would an exercise need to be to reveal meaningful judgment?
15. Which capabilities cannot be measured in a take-home assignment?
16. How would you detect work the student does not actually understand?
17. How should AI use be permitted, disclosed, or evaluated?
18. How long does a typical intern take to become useful?
19. What creates the largest onboarding delays?
20. Which capabilities are trainable, and which are difficult to teach?
21. Who defines the hiring bar?
22. Who would need to approve an external assessment or simulation?
23. What legal, confidentiality, or fairness concerns would arise?
24. What would make you refuse to use a system like this?

## Concept test

Only after discovery, say:

> We are considering a system where hiring managers define one difficult, safe, role-relevant work product; students complete it with explicit AI-use rules; and companies receive evidence-linked results rather than another resume pile.

Then ask:

25. What is wrong with this concept?
26. What would make the result untrustworthy?
27. Which part creates the most value?
28. Would you help define a rubric?
29. Would you review the strongest three to five submissions?
30. What would need to be true before you would interview a strong performer?
