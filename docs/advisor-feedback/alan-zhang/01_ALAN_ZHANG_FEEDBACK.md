# Alan Zhang Strategy Feedback

## Context

Feedback from Alan Zhang, a Columbia alumnus with experience at McKinsey and Citadel and current experience in the AI industry. The original message was sent through voice transcription. The material below preserves Alan's core arguments while removing transcription artifacts.

This is advisor input. It should be tested against customer evidence rather than treated as proven fact.

## Core arguments

### 1. Speak with hiring managers, not only recruiters

Hiring managers directly supervise interns and understand what their teams actually need. They are better positioned to define which technical capabilities predict success, what work products demonstrate aptitude, and why an intern succeeded or failed after joining.

### 2. Begin with one narrow, high-value internship category

Covenda does not need a large initial student network. It should choose one industry and role where students have strong incentives to participate and companies place unusually high value on exceptional talent.

Alan's strongest example is quantitative-finance software engineering because student demand is high, firms spend heavily on talent, and longer-horizon work may reveal ability that resumes and short tests miss.

### 3. Sell overlooked-talent discovery, not cost reduction

The company-side value proposition should focus on finding strong candidates that existing recruiting pipelines miss. For high-value firms, one exceptional hire may create enough value to justify substantial spending on discovery.

### 4. Build difficult, role-specific evidence

Covenda should not give students generic side-project suggestions. The work should be nontrivial, role-specific, difficult to fake, designed with hiring-manager input, and representative of real judgment and execution.

### 5. Expand from discovery into accelerated onboarding

Once Covenda understands what a role truly requires, it may build realistic simulations, pre-internship preparation, AI-enabled judgment exercises, and role-specific onboarding that helps interns become productive faster.

### 6. Use a prestige halo instead of forcing early network effects

A successful pilot with one respected firm or demanding role may create more credibility than a broad platform with shallow adoption.

## Strategic sequence implied by the feedback

```text
Choose one valuable role
→ interview hiring managers
→ define the real performance bar
→ create difficult role-specific evidence
→ surface overlooked candidates
→ track hiring outcomes
→ build accelerated onboarding
→ expand into adjacent roles and companies
```

## Unresolved questions

The feedback does not prove that quant firms will join an external pilot, managers will share enough information, realistic simulations can avoid confidentiality problems, or Covenda can navigate enterprise sales cycles. These questions require direct discovery.
