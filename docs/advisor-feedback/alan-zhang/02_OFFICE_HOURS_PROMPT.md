Covenda's long-term thesis is that students should be evaluated through demonstrated evidence rather than resume polish, keyword matching, prestige, or mass applications.

We have received substantial advisor feedback arguing that Covenda may be starting too broadly and speaking to the wrong company stakeholders. The main claims are:

1. Hiring managers should be the primary discovery audience, not recruiters.
2. Covenda does not need a large initial student network.
3. Quantitative-finance software engineering may be a strong initial wedge.
4. Covenda should sell overlooked-talent discovery, not recruiting cost reduction.
5. Covenda should help students demonstrate difficult, role-specific work.
6. Accelerated onboarding may be a natural second product.
7. Success with one prestigious firm may produce a halo effect.

Challenge Covenda's current strategy using this feedback. Do not automatically agree with the advisor.

For every conclusion, label it as one of:

- advisor-derived insight;
- supported by current Covenda evidence;
- model inference;
- unanswered market question.

Compare recruiters, campus recruiters, heads of talent, hiring managers, engineering managers, team leads, CTOs, founders, and learning-and-development leaders. Separate user, buyer, champion, evaluator, blocker, and beneficiary.

### B. Initial wedge comparison

1. Quant software engineering
2. Quantitative research
3. Investment banking
4. Consulting
5. Early-stage startup engineering
6. AI infrastructure engineering

### C. Product wedge comparison
