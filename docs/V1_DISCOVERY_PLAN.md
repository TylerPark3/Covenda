## The one question

> Does a CTO at a sub-50-person software company experience a gap between the candidates their
> process surfaces and the candidates who could succeed on their team, and can they name what
> would reveal it?

## ⚠ The 30-day bar is Gate 1, not interview count

`02_CEO_REVIEW.md:137`, binding and never superseded:

> **Gate 1 — demand (day 30).** 5 signed Talent Briefs, each with a real role, named evaluator,
> 5-day SLA. → Fail: **stop and re-segment before recruiting a single student.**

Revision 1 replaced this with "25 interviews completed, 4 Friday syntheses written" and never
named Gate 1 once. That swaps a commitment test for an activity test. **Interviews are the method.
Five signatures are the goal** (D21).

## Step zero, before any interview: Deal 000

⚠ `docs/pilot/BRIEF_GAP_LOG.md:32` designs a one-hour retrospective: re-run the closed deal's
scoping through `project-intake.js`, playing the company, feeding it their actual first message.
Count which of your real questions the engine asked.

It needs nobody's calendar, costs one hour, and **the table is currently blank.** Scheduling 20
interviews before running the free test the plan already designed is the error this review exists
to correct. Also record what the customer paid (D26).

## Allocation

| | Count | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews, software/AI | **20** | Test A1-A8 (see `V1_DECISION_LEDGER.md`) |
| Interview zero | 1 | The paying customer. Calibration, not a trigger |
| Quant probe, messages only | ⚠ **20** | Cold-tier response rate. **Not interviews. Non-decisive** (D17) |

⚠ **Interview count.** Revision 1 claimed 25 interviews from a cadence of 5 per week over four
weeks, which produces **20**.

⚠ **The probe is single-arm, and it is one-directional.** Revision 1 cut it to 5 messages, which
cannot distinguish a 30% response rate from a 10% one (P(≥2 of 5) is 47% versus 8%). Revision 2
restored 20 and specified "cold-tier on both arms" — but the software arm runs warm at tiers 0-5,
so a matched cold software arm would need ~20 *additional* messages that appear in no budget. That
was an unfunded requirement, and 20-vs-20 still has only **~35% power**, one-third of the ~62 per
arm the comparison actually needs.

**So it is not run as a comparison.** It is a single arm of 20 cold messages read against a fixed
benchmark, and it can only ever return one useful answer:

| Result | Reading |
|---|---|
| **~0/20** | 95% upper bound ≈ **14%**. This does exclude a software-like 30%. **Informative** |
| Anything else | 95% interval too wide to separate 10% from 30%. **Uninformative — record and move on** |

**What a positive result triggers: nothing, by design.** The vertical is closed by the product
contract (D16), not by a response rate. The probe exists only to stop the quant question being
re-opened on *no* evidence, and to record whether sub-75-person quant firms without recruiters are
reachable at all. If that is not worth 20 messages of a capacity-constrained week, **cut it** — the
plan does not depend on it. It is listed here so the choice is deliberate rather than silent.

## Interview zero — the customer who already paid

They bought the thing. Their words are the only demand vocabulary Covenda owns, and every
subsequent interview is calibrated against them.

Ask the Q9-addendum first: *"When you brought someone in, how long did it take to write down what
you actually wanted them to do, and what made that hard?"*

⚠ **Their answer does not stop the campaign.** Revision 1 made "if they say it was easy, stop"
a campaign-ending trigger on a single subjective recollection — the founder asking his own
customer, months later, whether the scoping *the founder personally conducted* was hard. Maximum
demand characteristics, zero independence.

**P1's real test is the 4× rule** (D19): if minutes-to-scoped-brief drops more than 4× between
deal 000 and deal 005, scoping was a learning tax and P1 is wrong. Numeric trigger, independent
measurement, already written down. Interview zero is context.

## Targets

CTO at sub-50 people, founding engineer, engineering manager, head of engineering, first-time
intern manager. Ranked by **combined authority**, not seniority: a person who decides, evaluates
and benefits beats three who each hold one piece.

0. The existing paying customer
1. Columbia alumni known personally
2. Alumni one introduction away
3. Former interns at target firms
4. Professors and club leaders with industry ties
5. Founders and investors connected to target firms
6. Cold LinkedIn

⚠ **The quant probe runs at tiers 6-7 only.** The software arm runs warm, so the two are not
comparable — which is why the probe is read single-arm against a fixed benchmark rather than
against the software rate (see Allocation).

## Cadence

20 personalised messages a week, 10 warm-introduction requests, 5 completed interviews, one
written synthesis every Friday.

**Track outreach-to-response ratio separately for software/AI and for the quant probe, cold-tier
only.** That comparison is D17's mechanism.

## ⚠ Capacity — the constraint revision 1 did not plan for

No V1 document contained the words runway, cash, hours, or semester. The plan starts 1 August; its
30-day window closes as the academic year begins, and the repo's own documents note that internship
postings land September-November. It asks one undergraduate to run, simultaneously: 20 messages a
week, 10 intro requests, 5 interviews, a weekly synthesis, the existing customer relationship, and
recruitment of 10-20 students.

`V1_PILOT.md` names the founder as the single point of failure and mitigates it with "Accepted.
Revisit at month 2." That is not a mitigation.

**The if-this-slips branch, pre-committed:**

| Trigger, measured Friday | Response |
|---|---|
| **Fewer than 2 signed briefs by day 20** | ⚠ **The leading indicator.** The funnel is wrong, not the segment. Change the ask — shorter, more concrete, name the role — do not wait for day 30 to discover this |
| Fewer than 3 interviews in a week | Drop the synthesis to bullet points. Do not drop interviews |
| Two consecutive weeks under 3 | Cut the interview target to 12. **Gate 1's bar and its day-30 date are unchanged** |
| Term starts and weekly hours fall below ~15 | Cut outreach to warm tiers 0-3 only. **Gate 1 still decides at day 30** |
| Deal 000 not run by day 3 | Stop outreach until it is. It costs one hour and gates the premise |

⚠ **Two triggers were removed as illegitimate.** Revision 2 wrote "extend to 45 days" and "run the
pilot with whatever briefs are signed" into a table sitting on the facing page of D21's claim that
**"Gate 1 stands, unmodified."** Both override the gate: one moves a day-30 binding date, the other
authorises the exact thing `02_CEO_REVIEW.md:139` forbids — *"stop and re-segment before recruiting
a single student."* A capacity plan may cut the method. It may not cut the gate. That was the same
supersession-by-omission this review exists to condemn, committed by the review itself.

### ⚠ August is the wrong month to measure demand (D27)

This is not a "cuts both ways" problem, as revision 2 called it. It is a systematic bias toward a
**false Gate 1 failure**, and the consequence of that failure is *stop and re-segment* — abandoning
a possibly-correct segment on the strength of the one month it is least observable.

Gate 1 requires five briefs each naming **a real open role**. The plan's own documents say
early-career postings land **September-November**. Measuring in August asks managers to sign
against roles that mostly do not exist yet.

**Resolution: move the window, never the bar.** The bar stays exactly as the binding log sets it —
five signed briefs, real role, named evaluator, 5-day SLA, decided on day 30, fail means stop.
What moves is when the 30-day clock starts.

| Period | Work |
|---|---|
| **August (day −30 to 0)** | Deal 000. Record what the customer paid. Add the tracker columns. Build the target list and warm-intro tiers. Interviews 1-5 — the no-pitch calibration ones. **No signature asks; no Gate 1 measurement** |
| **1-30 September (day 1-30)** | Interviews 6-20 with the signature ask on every qualifying one. **Gate 1 decides on day 30** |

This is an explicit decision with a stated reason, not a silent extension. It does not weaken Gate
1 — it is the difference between measuring demand and measuring the calendar. And it uses August
for the four things that need no counterparty anyway.

**What remains unresolved:** the founder is *least* available in exactly the window the managers
are *most* reachable. That conflict is real, it is not modelled, and no document here solves it.

## Progression

| Interviews | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 0 | The paying customer. Calibrate vocabulary. Record what they paid |
| 1-5 | Learn language. Do not pitch. Do not mention the product |
| **6-20** | Test recurrence — **and ask every manager who qualifies to sign a Talent Brief, from interview 6 onward** |
| 13-20 | Additionally: introduce one work-product concept and let them attack it |

⚠ **The signature ask moved from interviews 18-20 to interview 6.** Revision 2 restored Gate 1 —
five signed briefs by day 30 — while leaving the ask in the last three interviews of the campaign.
**Three asks cannot produce five signatures.** Gate 1 was unreachable by construction, and every
other fix in this revision leans on Gate 1.

From interview 6 there are ~15 asks for five signatures — a 33% close rate, which is demanding but
not arithmetically impossible. Interviews 1-5 still forbid pitching; that constraint is real and
is why the ask starts at 6 rather than 1.

⚠ **Condition 3 contamination.** D18's third condition requires the manager to describe a work
product *before Covenda proposes one*. From interview 13 onward Covenda introduces the concept, so
**condition 3 only counts when the manager describes it before that point in the conversation.**
Record the order in the tracker. Ask the open version first, every time, before any concept is
introduced.

## Script

`docs/advisor-feedback/alan-zhang/06_HIRING_MANAGER_INTERVIEW_SCRIPT.md`, plus four additions.

⚠ **The full instrument is 33 questions — a 75-90 minute call, five times a week, with people who
have no prior relationship to the founder.** That is not sustainable and it is the most likely
reason the cadence slips. **Run the 12-question core by default** (Q7-Q14, Q21, Q23, Q29, plus the
referral question below); go long only when a manager is clearly engaged and has the time.

**After Q9** — *"When you last brought in an intern, how long did it take you to write down what
you actually wanted them to do? What made that hard?"* Tests P1 directly. The only question in
the campaign that tests the founder's own finding rather than the advisor's claim.

**After Q17** — *"If a student used AI for most of the mechanical work but made every judgment
call themselves, is that a pass or a fail for you?"* Q17 asks how AI use should be evaluated; this
forces the ruling. The entire work-product design depends on the answer and nobody at Covenda
knows it.

**After Q24** — *"Who else should I be asking, and would you introduce me?"* Compounds the
warm-intro tiers.

⚠ **New, and it tests the existential risk** — *"How did your last two early-career hires actually
arrive?"* If the answer is "a referral" both times, from most managers, the real competitor wins
before Covenda is in the room. `02_CEO_REVIEW.md:155` classified **"Referrals win" as Existential**
and every V1 document had dropped it (D22, A7). Ask it every time. Record the answer verbatim.

## Do not pitch

Do not mention the compatibility engine, batch admission, the 374 scenarios, the evidence ladder,
or any number the system computes. Describing a scoring system to someone whose job is judging
people invites them to critique your algorithm instead of telling you about their problem.

## Thresholds (D18)

| Confirmations, of 20 | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **5+** | The gap is real and serveable | Proceed to Gate 1 signatures, then the pilot |
| **3-4** | Real signal, insufficient sample | **Extend by 15 interviews. Do not build** |
| **0-2** | Not real, or not here | Abandon and re-segment |

⚠ **"Unprompted" is dropped as unmeasurable.** Revision 1 required all five conditions confirmed
"independently and unprompted" — but the script prompts four of them directly (Q10, Q11-13, Q29,
Q28-30). Once Q10 has been asked, nothing after it is unprompted, and "which answers count" becomes
a judgement call by the person with the most to gain.

**A confirmation requires, recorded in the manager's own words:**

1. A **specific named instance** of a missed candidate — not agreement that it happens. "Yes,
   probably" does not count.
2. A capability they say a resume cannot show.
3. A work product **they describe concretely enough to build**, before Covenda proposes one.
4. Willingness to review results, with a stated timeframe.
5. The role recurring at a stated frequency.

Specificity is measurable. Spontaneity is not.

**Pre-committed before interview one.** A threshold set after seeing the data is not a threshold.

## Disconfirming evidence to watch for

Easy to explain away, which is why they are written down now:

- Screening is already fine; the constraint is headcount, not identification.
- ⚠ **The last two hires both came from referrals.** The status quo already works.
- They describe the gap but cannot define a work product. **The most dangerous outcome:** the pain
  is real and Covenda cannot serve it, which looks like encouragement until the pilot fails.
- Would use it, would not pay.
- Confidentiality blocks every realistic exercise.
- Nobody answers. Silence is data.

## Recording

`07_DISCOVERY_TRACKER.csv`. Per interview: segment, title, **company size**, introduction source
and tier, whether they named a **specific** missed candidate, whether they could define a work
product, whether they would review results, confidentiality objections, **how the last two hires
arrived**, whether a Talent Brief was signed, and **one verbatim quote**.

⚠ The tracker as shipped has **no verbatim-quote column and no company-size column**, both of which
this plan requires. Add them before interview one. The quote column is the most valuable: Covenda's
positioning is currently written in Covenda's words, and the gap between those and a hiring
manager's is usually where the product is.

## ⚠ What "signed" means

Gate 1 is the load-bearing measurement of this entire plan and revision 2 never defined its unit.
A Talent Brief counts toward Gate 1 only if it carries all five:

1. **A named role that is actually open** — title, team, and the manager's own one-line
   description of the work.
2. **A named evaluator** — a specific person, by name, who will review submissions. Not "someone
   on the team."
3. **A stated 5-day SLA** for that review.
4. **A written response from the evaluator's own account** agreeing to 1-3. Email counts; a
   verbal yes does not.
5. **A date.**

Anything less is a warm lead. Warm leads are worth recording and are **not** signatures. The
temptation to count four-out-of-five at day 29 is exactly what Gate 1 exists to resist.
