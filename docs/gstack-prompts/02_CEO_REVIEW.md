# CEO REVIEW

> Source: Covenda GStack Prompt Pack. Paste into the matching GStack skill.

Review the current Covenda V1 plan in SCOPE REDUCTION mode first, then use SELECTIVE EXPANSION only for ideas that directly strengthen the core transaction.

Read:

- `docs/COVENDA_GSTACK_CONTEXT.md`
- the approved `/office-hours` design document
- Chapters 3, 17, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, and 34 of `docs/COVENDA_MASTER_SPEC.md`
- the current repository and visible live product

The product thesis is:

Covenda helps students turn demonstrated work into a verified professional identity so relevant companies can discover and evaluate them through explainable evidence rather than mass applications.

Tagline:

Stop applying. Start getting discovered.

Determine the 10-star version of the first 90 days without allowing the long-term blueprint to become the V1 backlog.

Make explicit decisions on:

- the first student segment;
- the first company segment;
- the first use case;
- the first moment of value for each side;
- the role of the founder/operator;
- what must be manual;
- what should be productized;
- what should remain out of scope;
- what existing app concepts conflict with the thesis;
- the exact reason a student returns;
- the exact reason a company returns;
- whether the V1 proves discovery, evaluation, hiring, or all three;
- the minimum data Covenda must collect to improve future decisions.

Reject or defer features that do not contribute to a real 90-day transaction.

Non-negotiable cuts unless the repository already requires maintenance:

- no full 25-specialization system;
- no enterprise hiring intelligence;
- no universal ranking;
- no open direct messaging;
- no autonomous employment decisions;
- no broad social network;
- no complex token, points, or popularity system;
- no claims of improved hiring until outcome evidence exists.

Produce:

1. a one-sentence V1;
2. a one-paragraph product contract;
3. the primary and secondary user journeys;
4. the core transaction;
5. the 90-day operating model;
6. the minimum feature set;
7. explicit non-goals;
8. stage gates;
9. risks and assumptions;
10. a decision log that downstream GStack skills can treat as binding.
